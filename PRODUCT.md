# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Readers of a serious student newspaper: high-school and university students, teachers, parents, and admissions readers checking a writer's byline. They arrive on phones between classes and on laptops in the evening, and they read long (900–1,400 word) analytical essays end to end. The secondary "user" is the writers themselves — the site is their public portfolio, and it must make their work look as serious as it is.

## Product Purpose

The Argosy (theargosy.org) is an independent student newspaper written by Korean students filing from five time zones — politics, culture, economics, sustainability, health, psychology, sports, and a signature monthly Roundtable. Success = a visitor mistakes it, for a moment, for a professional broadsheet, then delights in discovering the writers are students. The archive (2023–present, 220+ pieces) is the asset; the site is its vessel.

## Brand Personality

Inky, seafaring, precocious. An argosy is a merchant ship — the paper's own metaphor ("a vessel built by many hands, carrying ideas across distance"). The visual voice is a classical broadsheet with a maritime spine: engraved ship seal, ink-navy and signal-flag section colors, confident serif typography, and one wink of youth (pixel-art staff avatars) that admits the crew is young without undermining the work.

## Anti-references

- The AI-default cream/beige "editorial minimal" landing page. This is a real newspaper, not an aesthetic of one.
- Medium/Substack genericism — centered column, no masthead identity, floating cards.
- SaaS landing grammar: hero metrics, icon-card grids, uppercase eyebrow labels.
- Tabloid/clickbait density; no infinite feeds, no engagement furniture.

## Design Principles

1. **The front page is an argument.** Hierarchy is editorial judgment made visible: one lead story, ranked sections, honest datelines — never a uniform card dump.
2. **Reading is the product.** Body measure, contrast, and rhythm outrank decoration everywhere; nothing may cost legibility.
3. **The ship is the brand.** Maritime spine (seal, ink, signal-flag section colors) carries identity; body surfaces stay ink-on-white.
4. **Broadsheet furniture, honestly used.** Rules, folios, kickers, drop caps, small-caps bylines — the traditional toolkit, deployed for real function.
5. **One wink, not a costume.** The pixel avatars are the single playful element; everything else keeps a straight face.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥4.5:1, metadata ≥4.5:1, large/labels ≥3:1. Full keyboard navigation with visible focus, skip link, semantic landmarks. `prefers-reduced-motion` honored (no parallax/scroll-jacking anywhere). Single light theme is a deliberate brand commitment (`color-scheme: light`). Alt text carries editorial voice.
