## 2. The Short Version

*Read this first. Older readers: read the full article too.*

When a phone battery dies or a laptop screen cracks, most people buy a new one. Fixing it is often too expensive, and small repair shops often can't get the parts, tools, or instructions from the company that made it.

The **Right to Repair** is a movement that wants to change that. Its idea is simple: if you buy something, you should be allowed to fix it yourself or take it to any repair shop you choose. Supporters want laws that make companies sell parts, tools, and repair manuals at a fair price.

This matters for the planet. The world throws away more than 50 million tons of electronics every year. Old gadgets contain harmful materials like lead and mercury, and valuable ones like gold and copper. When things last longer, there is less trash and less mining.

Companies say only their own workers should do repairs, to keep customers safe and protect their designs. Critics say the real reason is money: a phone that is hard to fix is more likely to be replaced.

Farmers, hospital technicians, and lawmakers in Europe and several U.S. states are now pushing for repair rights.

## For Teachers and Parents: Answer Key

**Word bank sentence:** Answers vary; check that the student uses e-waste to mean thrown-away electronics.

**1.** The idea that if you buy a product, you should be allowed to fix it yourself or take it to any repair shop you choose.

**2.** They often can't get the parts, tools, or repair information from the company that made it.

**3.** More than 50 million tons.

**4.** Harmful: lead, mercury, or flame retardants. Valuable: gold, silver, copper, or rare earth elements.

**5.** Companies: safety, data security, or protecting their designs. Critics: profit, because hard-to-fix products get replaced and repairs can be expensive.

**Ledger (sample answers):** Win: less e-waste, less mining, lower costs, people keep things longer. Cost: companies may earn less from repairs and new sales; a bad repair could be unsafe. Catch: companies must agree to share parts and manuals, which usually takes new laws.

**Think deeper:** Open answers. Strong answers connect "smart" to lasting, being fixable, and creating less waste. For farmers and hospitals, a broken machine can stop a harvest or delay patient care, so waiting for an official repair costs much more.

**Report:** Look for a clear answer, two reasons taken from the article, a fair statement of the other side, and a conclusion that matches the answer.
