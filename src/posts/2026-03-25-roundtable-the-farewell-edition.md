*Editor's Note: This is the last roundtable for the writers who built this paper. The Argosy continues — next month's masthead will introduce itself properly, and readers have already met much of it — but this particular table, in this particular configuration, convenes for the final time. They set their own house rule: no speeches. Ask each other the questions you never got around to. Lightly edited for length; the laughter has been left where it fell.*

**Minwoo Jung:** So. Three years, several hundred pieces, seven time zones of deadline complaints, and this is the final time I will get to say: welcome to the roundtable.

**Minwoo Jung:** House rule tonight is no speeches. Therefore, a question, to anyone brave enough: what is the piece you didn't write?

**Yonghyuk Choi:** Easy. The one where I admit I cried at a golf tournament.

**Yonghyuk Choi:** Tournaments, plural. The sports desk projects analytical detachment. The sports desk got misty at a Ryder Cup APOLOGY STATEMENT.

**Saerom Kim:** The piece I didn't write is the first draft of Part 1 of my series, which was terrible, and which said "as an objective observer" in the opening paragraph. I was never objective. It took me six parts to admit who I was writing about.

**Saerom Kim:** My question back: what did you all figure out embarrassingly late?

**Anthony Min:** That readers could tell when I didn't understand something. Early on I wrote around the holes with confident vocabulary. The comments found every hole. Markets are efficient. Comment sections, terrifyingly more so.

**Yehee Jung:** Embarrassingly late realization: my best pieces were the ones where I changed my mind while writing them. I used to treat that as evidence of poor planning. It took two years to see it was the entire job — the column was the lab notebook, not the press release.

**Jioh Lee:** I have only been at this table since November, so my late realization is very recent. I spent my first two pieces trying to sound like a writer. I spent the third one trying to say true things about the place I am from.

**Jioh Lee:** Only one of those approaches produced mail from strangers.

**Kyusung Lee:** Same seat, same timeline. What I learned embarrassingly late — like, February — is that "sports and society" isn't a beat. It's a permission slip. This paper let me write about faith and belonging with box scores as the door.

**Kyusung Lee:** I thought I was smuggling. Turns out the editors could see the smuggling the whole time and that was, apparently, the point.

**Minwoo Jung:** It was extremely visible smuggling. We approved every shipment.

**Yehee Jung:** My question, since no one has dared ask it in three years: did anyone actually read the group chat all the way through? Ever? Once?

**All:** [a silence the transcript cannot do justice to]

**Saerom Kim:** Moving on. Real question, real answers: what does this paper get to claim? When we're old and insufferable about having founded a student newspaper — what's the true sentence we're allowed to say?

**Minwoo Jung:** That we took the world seriously before anyone asked us to. Nobody assigned a group of teenagers the ceasefires and the central banks and each other's homesickness. We assigned ourselves. That is the entire claim, and I believe it suffices.

**Anthony Min:** Narrower claim: we showed our work. Wrong predictions left standing. Corrections in public. Confessions every December. I'm proudest of the graveyard of my bad calls.

**Yonghyuk Choi:** I'd claim the roundtable itself. Anyone can post takes. We practiced disagreeing — on the record, by name, with people we had to keep liking afterward. Do you know how rare that is on the internet we grew up on?

**Yonghyuk Choi:** And my closing question, because someone has to: what are you stealing from someone at this table?

**Kyusung Lee:** Saerom's habit of asking one question and then shutting up. It's interview technique and it's also, I've decided, a theology.

**Jioh Lee:** Anthony's rule. Follow the incentives. I intend to apply it to every institution I attend for the rest of my life. Including, prophylactically, this newspaper.

**Yehee Jung:** Minwoo's discipline — what we know, what we feel, what we hope, in that order. I came for the science. That sentence structure is what I'm leaving with.

**Saerom Kim:** Yonghyuk's conviction that the game is never just the game. It transferred to everything. The potluck is never just the potluck.

**Anthony Min:** Yehee's lab-notebook honesty. If I ever write again, the draft will say "here is where I changed my mind" in the actual text.

**Minwoo Jung:** And I am stealing from the two newest members, which ought to embarrass the rest of us. Jioh and Kyusung arrived in November and immediately wrote about home. No throat-clearing. No proving-ground pieces. It took me two years of geopolitics to write one honest paragraph about Seoul.

**Minwoo Jung:** The kids, as the saying goes, are faster.

**Minwoo Jung:** Last order of business. Next month this table belongs to others — you know their names from these pages, and the paper is in alarmingly good hands; the handover letter will do the formalities in June. Spring is doing its usual thing where several of us learn, via envelope, what happens next. Watch this space.

**Minwoo Jung:** Final thoughts. One sentence each. No crying. Yonghyuk is exempt from the second rule for medical reasons.

---
### Final Thoughts

**Minwoo Jung:** We were seven strangers in five time zones who decided the world was our beat — and the great surprise of my adolescence is that the deciding was all it took.

**Yehee Jung:** Somewhere in three years of deadlines I stopped writing to seem smart and started writing to get it right, and that is the entire education, and it happened here.

**Saerom Kim:** I came to this paper a newcomer documenting a community and leave having been documented into one — every door I knocked on turned out to be mine.

**Yonghyuk Choi:** [exercising the medical exemption] Box score reads: three years, one family, no losses that stayed losses.

**Anthony Min:** Best trade I will ever make: three years of unpaid labor for a lifetime supply of people who tell me when I'm wrong.

**Jioh Lee:** Five months, and I found the thing I had been between countries looking for — it turns out "where are you really from" can be answered with a masthead.

**Kyusung Lee:** Under the lights on Friday, in the pews on Sunday, and around this table on deadline — same congregation, and I got to belong to all three.

*To every reader who argued with us in the comments, corrected our numbers, and came back anyway: this paper was always a conversation, and you were the other half. Keep reading. The Argosy continues. — The founding table*
