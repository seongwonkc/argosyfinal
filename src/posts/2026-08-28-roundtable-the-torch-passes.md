*Editor's Note: With the school year beginning, the new editorial board convenes its first September table — inheriting a tradition, setting its own agenda, and, per house rules, making predictions it will be forced to grade in December. Lightly edited for length and clarity.*

**Ethan Yang:** Fourth year of The Argosy, first September of the new table. The founders had a rule for this meeting: declare your beat's big question for the year, publicly, so December can embarrass you. I'll chair and go last. Gawon — Psychology Editor, officially, as of this summer. Your year?

**Gawon Ryu:** My question for the year: the verdicts are coming. Australia's under-16 social media ban turns one in December — the first real data from the biggest experiment ever run on teenage attention, which I covered when the switch flipped. The AI-companion reckoning is visibly next in the legislatures. And the youth mental-health numbers, which drove all of it, will tell us whether any needle moved. My beat spent years in the argument phase. This is the year evidence starts arriving, and my public commitment stands: I report the results even if they dismantle my priors. *Especially* if.

**David Lee:** Sustainability desk, three watch items, ranked by my honest excitement. Three: the fusion switch-on campaign — the magnets I wrote about last week go operational next year, and the "thirty years away forever" joke faces its exam. Two: whether the grid survives its strangest year — AI demand still exploding, an oil shock still rippling out of the Gulf, and the first full year of American climate law running without its keystone. One, and I'm not being cute: air conditioners. The efficiency standards moving through a dozen governments after this record summer are the least glamorous story I cover and might be the biggest emissions win of the decade. My December embarrassment will be if nothing happens and I hyped ceiling appliances.

**Patrick Kim:** Culture desk. My year is one question wearing many costumes: *who owns the machine-made?* The AI copyright wars I covered with Sora went from skirmish to global litigation in ten months; the deals being signed this fall between studios and AI platforms will set the terms for a generation. Plus the small matter of the most delayed video game in history allegedly, finally, actually arriving in November — which will either be the biggest entertainment launch ever recorded or my beat's funniest December confession. I've pre-drafted both columns. That's called preparation.

**Olivia Kim:** Science and health, and I get the sobering slot. My year-question: does the United States keep its measles elimination status? The arithmetic I wrote about in July is pointing the wrong way, and the decision gets made not in December but in thousands of school-entry forms being filed right now, this month, in every district in the country. On the hopeful side of my ledger: the first full year of the obesity pill era, the gene-therapy pipeline moving from miracle stories to approval calendars, and — personal watchlist — whether the fusion people and the AI-drug-discovery people both survive contact with their own schedules. Biology grades harshly. I'll be in the front row with the rubric.

**Ethan Yang:** Chair's turn, then. My question for the year is the one the whole masthead orbits without saying: *does the center hold?* Every desk at this table is covering the same story in different clothes — systems built for a stable world (grids, schedules, treaties, school calendars, immune thresholds, attention spans) being stress-tested by a world that stopped being stable. Some are adapting brilliantly. Some are visibly failing the test. Our job, year four, is telling readers which is which, honestly, beat by beat. Also — housekeeping — the founders' traditions all survive: the year-end confession table, the debate format, the roundtable question to readers. Some things you don't modernize.

**Patrick Kim:** Speaking of the founders — should we report on the diaspora? The group chat remains extremely active. Minwoo has already sent the table three articles about campus election coverage with the message "your move." Saerom mailed the office a jar of pepper paste with unpacking instructions. Yonghyuk's voice memos have not decreased in length or emotional range.

**Gawon Ryu:** And they read everything. Within hours. It's the most benevolent surveillance state in journalism.

**Ethan Yang:** As it should be. Final thoughts, then — first September, one sentence each, and per the oldest house rule: no speeches, just the true thing.

---
### Final Thoughts

**Gawon Ryu:** The argument years are ending and the evidence years are beginning — on my beat and, I suspect, on everyone's; may we all be caught being honest.

**David Lee:** The transition doesn't need cheerleaders or doomsayers this year; it needs scorekeepers, and this desk is keeping score.

**Patrick Kim:** Culture's big question used to be "what does it mean"; this year it's "who owns it" — and I intend to follow the receipts.

**Olivia Kim:** The thresholds don't negotiate — measles, sleep, heat, all of it — so this column will keep publishing the thresholds until the world stops testing them.

**Ethan Yang:** Seven founders proved that deciding to take the world seriously is the whole secret; the five of us just have to keep deciding it, weekly, in public, starting now.

*New year, new table, same ship. What should The Argosy be watching this year? The comments are open — and to the founding seven, reading this from five campuses: your move.*
