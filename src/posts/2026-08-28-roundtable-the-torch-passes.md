*Editor's Note: With the school year beginning, the expanded editorial board convenes its first September table. Three staff writers step up to lead new desks — Peter Lee (Health), Isaac Kim (Education), and Ejin Chung (Science & Technology) — and the politics desk welcomes its first new writer of the post-founder era, Doyeon Kim. Per house rules, everyone makes predictions December will be allowed to grade. Lightly edited for length and clarity.*

**Ethan Yang:** Fourth year of The Argosy. First September of the new table. And the biggest masthead this paper has ever run — nine of us now, which means the group chat has achieved what our founders would call critical mass and what I call a moderation problem.

**Ethan Yang:** Formalities first, joyfully. Peter, Isaac, and Ejin have been writing for us since last year; as of this term they run their own desks — Health, Education, and Science & Technology. And Doyeon joins as our new politics writer, first of the post-founder era.

**Ethan Yang:** House rule for this meeting, inherited and non-negotiable: declare your beat's big question for the year, publicly, so December can embarrass you. Veterans first. Gawon.

**Gawon Ryu:** My year in one word: verdicts. Australia's under-16 ban turns one in December — the first real data from the biggest experiment ever run on teenage attention. The AI-companion reckoning is visibly next in the legislatures. And the youth mental-health numbers will start telling us whether any needle moved.

**Gawon Ryu:** My beat spent years in the argument phase. This is the year evidence starts arriving. My standing commitment: I report the results even if they dismantle my priors. (*Especially* if.)

**David Lee:** Sustainability desk, three watch items, ranked by honest excitement.

**David Lee:** Three: fusion's switch-on campaign — the magnets go operational next year, and the "thirty years away forever" joke faces its exam. Two: whether the grid survives its strangest year — AI demand still exploding, an oil shock still rippling out of the Gulf, the first full year of American climate law without its keystone.

**David Lee:** One, and I'm not being cute: air conditioners. The efficiency standards moving through a dozen governments after this record summer might be the biggest emissions win of the decade. If nothing happens and I hyped ceiling appliances, December can have me.

**Patrick Kim:** Culture desk. My year is one question in many costumes: *who owns the machine-made?* The AI copyright wars went from skirmish to global litigation in ten months, and the deals being signed this fall will set the terms for a generation.

**Patrick Kim:** Also the most delayed video game in history allegedly, finally, actually arrives in November. Which will either be the biggest entertainment launch ever recorded or my funniest December confession. I have pre-drafted both columns. That's called preparation.

**Olivia Kim:** Science and health writer, sobering slot, as usual. My year-question: does the United States keep its measles elimination status? The arithmetic I wrote about in July is pointing the wrong way — and the decision is being made right now, this month, in school-entry forms in every district in the country.

**Olivia Kim:** Hopeful side of my ledger: the gene-therapy pipeline moving from miracle stories to approval calendars. Biology grades harshly. I'll be in the front row with the rubric.

**Ethan Yang:** And now the new desks. Peter — Health Editor. You've been our quietest byline for a year. Not anymore. What's your year?

**Peter Lee:** Happy to be loud on schedule. My question: does the pill era actually reach people? We now have obesity medicine at drugstore prices, Medicare coverage starting, government price deals — the whole access architecture I've been writing about since last fall.

**Peter Lee:** On paper, the barriers fell. My year is checking whether they fell in practice — who actually gets the prescription, who's still outside the pharmacy looking in. Access is my desk's whole thesis: a cure that doesn't reach people is a press release.

**Isaac Kim:** Education desk, and my year-question writes itself: the first full admissions cycle of the testing restoration. The retreat from test-optional is complete at most selective schools now, and a generation that planned around one set of rules is applying under another.

**Isaac Kim:** Second watch item: every school on Earth is writing its AI-homework policy this fall, mostly in a panic, mostly copying each other. Somebody should read them all side by side. Unfortunately for my sleep schedule, that somebody is me.

**Ejin Chung:** Science & Technology desk, thrilled to exist officially. My year: the season the lab bets pay out or don't. AI-designed drugs are in human trials. The quantum computing world is still spending its Nobel glow. A rocket company had the largest IPO in history and then the most interesting six weeks of any stock alive.

**Ejin Chung:** My desk's rule, posted in advance: I cover results, not demos. If it only works in the keynote, it goes in the December confessions.

**Doyeon Kim:** And the rookie goes last, which is correct. I inherited a desk with an intimidating archive — I've read all of it, including the farewell column where my predecessor said the desk belongs to whoever keeps listening.

**Doyeon Kim:** So, listening: my year is the midterms. November 3rd. First national verdict on this era, Senate maps, all of it — and I'm making zero predictions about results, because I read what December does to people at this table.

**Doyeon Kim:** My actual question is a listener's question: whether the youth-revolt energy this paper has tracked from Kathmandu to New York shows up in an American midterm, or stays outside the ballot box. Ask me in December.

**Ethan Yang:** Zero predictions in a predictions meeting. The politics desk learns fast.

**Ethan Yang:** Chair's turn, briefly. My question for the year is the one every desk at this table now shares without saying it: does the center hold? Grids, schedules, treaties, school calendars, immune thresholds, attention spans — systems built for a stable world, stress-tested by one that stopped being stable. Some are adapting brilliantly. Some are visibly failing. Year four's job is telling readers which is which, honestly, desk by desk.

**Ethan Yang:** Housekeeping: the founders' traditions all survive. December confessions. The debate format. The reader question at the end.

**Patrick Kim:** Speaking of the founders — status report? The alumni group chat remains *extremely* active. Minwoo has sent three articles about campus election coverage captioned "your move." Saerom mailed the office a jar of pepper paste with unpacking instructions.

**Gawon Ryu:** And they read everything within hours. It's the most benevolent surveillance state in journalism.

**Doyeon Kim:** Confirmed. I posted my first draft link and got four comments before my roommate finished brushing her teeth.

**Ethan Yang:** As designed. Final thoughts, all nine — one sentence, and per the oldest house rule: no speeches, just the true thing.

---
### Final Thoughts

**Gawon Ryu:** The argument years are ending and the evidence years are beginning — on my beat and, I suspect, on everyone's; may we all get caught being honest.

**David Lee:** The transition doesn't need cheerleaders or doomsayers this year. It needs scorekeepers. This desk keeps score.

**Patrick Kim:** Culture's question used to be "what does it mean"; this year it's "who owns it" — and I intend to follow the receipts.

**Olivia Kim:** The thresholds don't negotiate — measles, sleep, heat, all of it — so this column will keep publishing the thresholds until the world stops testing them.

**Peter Lee:** Medicine's breakthroughs are upstream; my desk lives downstream, where people either get them or don't — and downstream is where the year will be decided.

**Isaac Kim:** Every education system on Earth is rewriting its rules at once, and somebody should file the comparison — neatly, with citations.

**Ejin Chung:** Results, not demos. Ask me in December which was which.

**Doyeon Kim:** I inherited a desk that listens; this year I find out what American voters say when someone actually does.

**Ethan Yang:** Seven founders proved that deciding to take the world seriously is the whole secret; the nine of us just have to keep deciding it, weekly, in public, starting now.

*New year, new table, same ship. What should The Argosy be watching this year? The comments are open — and to the founding seven, reading this from five campuses: your move.*
